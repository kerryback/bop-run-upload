\subsection*{3b: the search}
The original three-type economies and the levers tried on the way to the three in Section 3b.

The same $y$ with heterogeneity in what is exposed to it: three types of firms with exposures 0.01 / 0.035 / 0.07 (BGN) and 0.02 / 0.07 / 0.14 (KP14), level-compensated so that value at $y=0$ does not reveal the type; and in GS21, where types never produced room (Appendix A3), the product of standardized productivity and the standardized coupon written into the production function with coefficient 0.10. Exposure is then a product: type $\times$ project mix (BGN), type $\times$ growth-option share (KP14), productivity $\times$ leverage (GS21), each factor revealed by a different characteristic.

\noindent\textbf{Verdict.} Every interaction economy has a positive mean gap with most seeds positive ($t$ of 2.7 to 5.1 across seeds), at room of 16 to 47\% of the linear ceiling. The gaps are statistically clear and economically modest: 2 to 4\% of the linear Sharpe at these three representatives; the scaled GS21 versions in the appendix reach 11 to 22\%. The premium is multiplicatively separable in the ranks, $f(A)\,g(B)$; a linear model in the ranks fits only additive functions, random features span products, and DKKM recovers 15 to 30\% of the room as a gap. Going from 3a to 3b changes nothing but the second dimension of heterogeneity, and that is what creates the gap. The GS21 product ladder (Appendix A4) shows the room hump-shaped in the product's size and the gap rising to $+0.08$. The GS21 product is imposed: the coupon multiplies the exposure inside the production function. Its structural counterpart, where the exposure $0.10\,\tilde z\,y$ sits in cash flows and leverage acts only through the equity claim, was tested by fixing each firm's capital structure as a type drawn independently of productivity (\texttt{gvyzlf}: coupons 0.16, 0.42 and 0.68 per unit capital, debt-to-equity 0.25 to 3.25, no refinancing; four variants in the appendix). The interaction is there, the true premium's slope in leverage flips sign across profitability quintiles, but the equity claim amplifies the $y$ premium by only 20 to 40\% around a large additive productivity part, so room stays at 6 to 8\% of the linear ceiling and the gap at $+0.005$ ($t$ 1.8). The imposed product has no additive part, which is where its 43\% comes from. Three levers on \texttt{gvyzlf} fail to change this (appendix). Valuing the debt as riskless (\texttt{gvyzlr}) leaves every number unchanged, so the debt was not absorbing $y$ risk; a more persistent $y$ (\texttt{gvyzlp}, $\kappa_y$ 0.10) halves $\mathrm{SR}_{\max}$ and the interaction with it; and cutting the price of the aggregate productivity shock from 0.5 to 0.1 (\texttt{gvyzlg}, with its own benchmark \texttt{gsbg1} and 3a comparator \texttt{gvyz10g}) lowers the market's share from 50\% to 27\% but leaves the linear ceiling at 0.72, because the base is the priced productivity level itself rather than the market: room 9\%, gap $-0.006$. The interaction's own Sharpe is 0.31 to 0.33 in all of them, the size seen in KP14's and BGN's 3b economies (table column \emph{interaction SR}); what differs is the base it sits on. Two further wirings close the question from the other side: fixing the product's sign with a leverage-level term (\texttt{gvyp10c}, exposure $0.10\,\tilde b(\tilde z+2)$) hands the market a large negative premium and leaves room of 1\%, and KP14's own recipe, three latent exposure types times three fixed leverage types (\texttt{gvytl}, and \texttt{gvytlg} at a fifth of the aggregate shock's price), gives the market 72 to 84\% of the attainable Sharpe and room of 1 to 3\%. Every exposure with a nonzero cross-sectional mean is carried by GS21's equal-weighted market; the product is the one wiring whose mean is zero, which is why it alone has room.

\noindent\textbf{The sign of the type loading.} The dispersion bound in the summary is not what sets room, and the reduced form said so before the structural runs did. With one priced factor and $N=500$, any linear-in-characteristics portfolio whose covariance with the exposure is not zero mimics the factor and diversifies the rest, so it takes the factor's whole Sharpe: room is all or nothing, and it is all only when the exposure is orthogonal to every linear function of the characteristics. The sign ladder of the reduced form (appendix, \texttt{lsh} rows) shows this directly: a product of two mean-zero latent states has room of $+0.39$ to $+0.42$, and shifting either factor's mean by one standard deviation, or more, or replacing the product by a one-signed ratio (\texttt{lrt}), sends room to 0.000 to 0.005 at the same $\mathrm{SR}_{\max}$. In the structural models the type loading's cross-sectional mean is that shift. Mirroring vyg25's ladder to six types of equal share with loadings $\pm0.02$, $\pm0.07$, $\pm0.14$ (\texttt{vyg25s}, level-compensated as vyg25, price 2.5) raises KP14's room from $+0.082$ to $+0.349$, two and a quarter times a linear ceiling that falls from 0.346 to 0.174 (a mean-zero type leaves no $\bar b\times$growth-option piece for a book-to-market sort to hold), $\mathrm{SR}_{\max}$ from 0.495 to 0.575, and DKKM's fair gap from $+0.015$ to $+0.181$ ($t$ 17, ten of ten seeds; DKKM 0.373 against FMR's 0.102 and the linear model's 0.153). The market's share stays at 11\%. BGN moves the same way but less far: \texttt{bvychs} (bvych0's ladder mirrored, $\pm0.01$, $\pm0.035$, $\pm0.07$, level-compensated) has room of 20\% of the linear ceiling against 16\% and a fair gap of $+0.026$ ($t$ 4.9, nine of ten) against $+0.008$, with the linear ceiling unchanged (0.341 to 0.349) and $\mathrm{SR}_{\max}$ lower (0.576 to 0.510). Why BGN's linear ceiling holds where KP14's halves is not established; BGN's compensation is weaker ($\theta_f$ 1 to 1.60 against 1 to 3.36) and its $y$ is a bounded chain, so part of the type may stay visible in the characteristics. Hiding the type is what makes the sign matter: \texttt{vyg25u}, vyg25 without the level compensation so that the type shows in value at $y=0$, has vyg25's returns exactly ($\mathrm{SR}_{\max}$ 0.495 to every digit; only the characteristics change) and loses the gap ($-0.002$, $t$ $-0.5$, three of ten) and a fifth of the room, and \texttt{bvysg}, the earlier BGN signed ladder without compensation, has a negative gap where \texttt{bvychs} has a positive one. The capturable gap is then the Sharpe of the part of the exposure that no linear function of the characteristics spans, not a dispersion ratio, and a mean-zero type loading hidden by the level compensation puts nearly all of KP14's $y$ premium there. GS21 does not follow. \texttt{gvy25s}, gvy25's three level types mirrored to six of equal share with $b_f\in\{\pm0.015,\pm0.05,\pm0.10\}$, level-compensated (bv\_comp 1.2) at the same price, raises GS21's room from $+0.014$ to $+0.062$ (2\% to 11\% of the linear ceiling) and halves the market's share of $\mathrm{SR}_{\max}$ (84\% to 50\%), but the linear ceiling keeps 88\% of $\mathrm{SR}_{\max}$ (0.558 of 0.632, against 0.684 of 0.739 in gvy25), and DKKM does not take what is left: the fair gap is $-0.012$ ($t$ $-4.4$, two of ten seeds positive; DKKM 0.549 against FMR's 0.541 and the fair linear model's 0.561), where the prediction was $+0.02$ to $+0.06$. The explanation this suggests, not yet tested, is the one thing that separates the three models: GS21's firm chooses its coupon, investment and default given its type, so a negative cash-flow loading shows in the chosen leverage and in book-to-market even though the level compensation hides it in value at $y=0$, and the linear span holds most of the exposure; KP14's firm chooses nothing and BGN's only whether to invest, and the order of the three rooms (226\%, 20\%, 11\%) is the order of how much the firm decides.

\noindent\textbf{The strength of the level compensation.} Two ladders test whether the compensation, which sets how visible the type is, moves room. In KP14 it changes the characteristics and nothing else: \texttt{vyg25s6} and \texttt{vyg25s18} (bv\_comp 0.6 and 1.8) have \texttt{vyg25s}'s returns exactly, seed by seed. The type's visibility moves a lot ($R^2$ of $b_f$ on the ranks 0.67--0.74 at 0.6, through book-to-market; 0.88--0.92 at 1.2; 0.95--0.97 at 1.8, through size), the linear ceiling barely does (0.21, 0.17, 0.21), room is 147\%, 226\% and 167\% of it, and the fair gap is $+0.142$ ($t$ 24), $+0.181$ and $+0.185$ ($t$ 8.7), five of five seeds at both new points. The premium stays hump-shaped in $b_f$ because the returns do not change, and the hump is the room; how visible the type is does not matter. In BGN the compensation does change returns, and weakening it goes the other way: \texttt{bvychs4}, \texttt{bvychs6}, \texttt{bvychs8} (bv\_comp 0.4, 0.6, 0.8) make the type more visible through size ($R^2$ 0.89 at 0.4, 0.76 at 0.8) with the premium monotone in $b_f$, room falls from 20\% to 12\%, 8\% and 7\% of a linear ceiling that rises from 0.35 to 0.52 and 0.59, and the fair gap is $-0.004$, $+0.001$, $+0.009$, none distinguishable from zero (five, three and three seeds, the rest stopped at the oracle). The spec's prediction, a hidden sign near 0.6 lifting room above 50\%, is falsified.

\noindent\textbf{GS21 with its capital structure fixed.} \texttt{gvytls} takes \texttt{gvytl}'s chassis (three coupons, 0.15 / 0.40 / 0.70 per unit capital, fixed for life and drawn independently of the type) and mirrors the exposure ladder as \texttt{gvy25s} does, eighteen mean-zero types, to test whether the firm's coupon choice is what reveals the sign in GS21. It is not: room is 8\% of the linear ceiling (eight oracle seeds) against \texttt{gvy25s}'s 11\%, and the fair gap $-0.007$ ($t$ $-1.0$, one of three; the other seven stopped). The type stays visible ($R^2$ 0.83--0.84; ROE $+0.68$, size $+0.67$, book-to-market $-0.63$), within each coupon the mean return rises with $b_f$, and the most exposed levered firms select themselves out: in each of three seeds none of the $b_f=-0.10$, coupon-0.70 firms and at most five of about thirty $b_f=-0.05$, coupon-0.70 firms survive into the panel. The prediction (room 15--25\%) is falsified.

\noindent\textbf{A hump outside KP14, larger loadings, stronger compensation.} The decomposition of each type's premium on the market and on $y$ (\texttt{variants/hump\_decomp.py}) says why only KP14's premium is hump-shaped: in \texttt{vyg25s} the market beta rises from 0.71 to 1.27 across the ladder while the residual premium falls from $+0.10$ to $-0.23$\% a month, two opposed channels whose sum peaks inside; in \texttt{bvychs} both rise with $b_f$, in \texttt{gvy25s} they oppose but the $y$ channel is ten times the market channel. Seven economies tried to build the second channel into BGN and GS21 and none did: a pure curvature of GS21's productivity loading without $y$ (\texttt{gxq35}, \texttt{gxq70}) gives room of 1 to 3\% and no gap; \texttt{gvy25s} with its level shifts kept and the $y$ price cut (\texttt{gvy25sq}, \texttt{gvy25sh}: $\gamma_v$ 0.25 and 0.5, bv\_comp 12 and 9) makes the premium non-monotone but five times smaller, room 1 to 3\%, gap $-0.004$ and $-0.008$. BGN's compensation ladder continued in both directions (\texttt{bvychsn5}, \texttt{bvychs18}, \texttt{bvychs25}, \texttt{bvychs30}, \texttt{bvychs35}: bv\_comp $-0.5$, 1.8, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5) never produces a hump, the premium staying monotone in $b_f$, but room rises from 20\% of the linear ceiling at 1.2 and 27\% at 1.8 to 67\% at 2.5 and 3.0 and 66\% at 3.5 ($+0.16$ to $+0.19$), cross-sectional nonlinearity in how the compensated characteristics map to $b_f$ (the bins basis alone reaches 0.36 against a linear 0.21). DKKM takes part of it: the fair gap is $+0.048$ ($t$ 3.7, nine of ten) at 2.5 and $+0.063$ ($t$ 5.6, ten of ten) at 3.0, the largest BGN gaps of the campaign, where 1.8 gives $+0.006$ and $-0.5$ gives $-0.005$ despite 35\% room.

\noindent\textbf{The size of the loading.} The one lever that moved the gap is the size of the sign-switching loading, and only in KP14. \texttt{vyg25sx2} doubles \texttt{vyg25s}'s loadings ($\pm0.04$, $\pm0.14$, $\pm0.28$; $\theta_f$ 1 to 11.2): SR$_{\max}$ 0.575 to 0.640, linear ceiling 0.174 to 0.158, room $+0.437$ (343\% of the ceiling), fair gap $+0.245$ ($t$ 11.1, ten of ten; DKKM 0.456 against the linear model's 0.134) against $+0.181$; the hump deepens (seed 4: $-0.04$, $+0.43$, $+0.60$, $+0.58$, $+0.43$, $+0.32$\% a month across the ladder, the residual channel $+0.25$ to $-0.27$ against a market beta of 0.58 to 1.36). Tripling (\texttt{vyg25sx3}; $\theta_f$ 1 to 37) is past the peak: the level spread begins to show linearly in size, the linear ceiling rises to 0.248, room is $+0.364$ (175\%) and the gap $+0.218$ ($t$ 9.7). BGN and GS21 do not follow: \texttt{bvychsx2} and \texttt{bvychsx3} raise room from $+0.068$ to $+0.089$ and $+0.081$ with gaps of $+0.007$ and $+0.002$, and \texttt{gvy25sx2} makes the type linearly visible (linear ceiling 0.72 of an SR$_{\max}$ of 0.82, gap $+0.0005$).

\noindent\textbf{Interaction and non-monotone exposures in every model.} The last round asked for six economies, in each model one whose gap comes from an interaction of characteristics and one whose exposure is non-monotone, each with a fair gap of 0.1 a year ($+0.029$ monthly). All six exist; the three non-monotone economies are in the next subsection. KP14 non-monotone: the hump across the size ladder, \texttt{vyhx3} ($+0.212$; the sign ladder's emergent hump \texttt{vyg25sx2}, $+0.245$, is its cousin here). BGN interaction: \texttt{bvychs35} ($+0.071$; the compensation ladder 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.5 / 4.0 gives $+0.048$ / $+0.063$ / $+0.071$ / $+0.059$, peaking at 3.5 because past it the level spread makes the type a size factor the Fama--French benchmark holds). GS21 interaction: the imposed product \texttt{gvyp15} ($+0.078$) and, with a story, the industry sign times the firm's productivity (\texttt{gvysz2}, $+0.059$, $t$ 11.5, ten of ten; \texttt{gvysz1} $+0.053$; the paragraph below); strong compensation on the mirrored ladder (\texttt{gvy25sc25}) gives room of 31--34\% and no gap ($-0.023$). GS21 and BGN non-monotone: \texttt{gvyzzh10} ($+0.080$) and \texttt{bvyhx4} ($+0.119$), next subsection. KP14 interaction was the last filled, and ends as the largest interaction gap in any model: the sign-switching ladders' premium is hump-shaped whatever the compensation (\texttt{vyg25su}, \texttt{vyg25sux2}: bv\_comp 0, the parents' returns to the digit, gaps $+0.116$ and $+0.191$), the positive ladder's levers leave room at \texttt{vyg25}'s 19--28\% (loadings $\times2$ / $\times3$, $\gamma_v$ 3.5, bv\_comp 2.5: 14--26\%), and a new channel, the $y$ exposure on the firm's profitability deviation $b_f(\varepsilon-1)$ (\texttt{bv\_on\_eps}; \texttt{vyge}, \texttt{vygel}, \texttt{vygesm}: the type hidden, revealed through size, or mirrored), leaves room at 7--27\%: with positive loadings the product is a ROE sort with a type-dependent slope, and the mirrored version is capped at $\pm0.15$ by the positivity of project values under the risk-neutral measure. The bounded exposure fills it: with $e^{b_f y}$ on the profitability deviation replaced by $h_b(y) = c/(1+(c-1)e^{-cby/(c-1)})$ ($h(0)=1$, slope $b$ at zero, range $(0,c)$; \texttt{bv\_eps\_cap}, the pricing equation unchanged but for $h$ on its right-hand side), the claim is bounded at $c$ times its unexposed value and the mirrored ladder runs at loadings to $\pm1.2$ with positive project values. Room and gap rise together along the family (\texttt{vygelb} $\pm0.1/0.25/0.45$, cap 2, price 2.5: room 21--31\%, $+0.002$; \texttt{vygelb7} $\pm0.2/0.45/0.7$: 27--45\%, $+0.005$; \texttt{vygelb7g}, the same at price 4.0: 33--62\%, $+0.014$; \texttt{vygelbw} $\pm0.3/0.7/1.2$: 30--73\%, $+0.022$; \texttt{vygelbx} $\pm0.5/1.2/2.0$: 28--70\%, $+0.030$, the width saturating), and \texttt{vygelbc} ($\pm0.3/0.7/1.2$ at cap 2.5, price 4.0) has room 40--90\% and a fair gap of $+0.037$ ($t$ 6.6, ten of ten; DKKM 0.272 against 0.235): the type's sign read from size, the firm's deviation from ROE, their product spanned by no linear function of the characteristics. The profitability claim is a large share of value only for the negative loadings ($A_1/A_0$ 0.7--0.9 against 0.05--0.15), the room converts at about 17\% of its size against 45\% for the humps, and the levers are the cap (above 2.7 project values turn negative), the price of $y$ risk (\texttt{vygelbc6} at 6.0: room 48--88\%, $+0.049$, $t$ 5.8, ten of ten) and the ladder width, in that order; the strongest lever of all is the profitability dispersion, since the exposure $b_f(\varepsilon-1)h'(y)$ scales with it: at $\sigma_\varepsilon$ 0.3 (\texttt{vygelbcs}, cap 2.5, price 4.0) the absolute room is 0.15--0.30 in Sharpe (58--119\%) and the fair gap $+0.091$ ($t$ 12.3, ten of ten; DKKM 0.388 against 0.297; seeds $+0.041$ to $+0.131$), three times the bar. The reduced form agrees: a factor on the square of one partly hidden latent gives room $+0.40$ and a fair gap of $+0.37$ ($t$ 11) when the latent is book-to-market (\texttt{lsqb\_a0\_n0}), any shift of its mean adds a linear part that sends room to 0.002, and the same square on the size latent has the room and no fair gap because the value-weighted market inside the Fama--French set holds $\mathrm{size}^2$.

\noindent\textbf{Leverage as the amplifier in GS21.} Financial leverage, the more natural amplifier, does not work as a type: two industries (gs\_level 0.8 / 1.2) times three fixed coupons with the exposure proportional to the coupon (\texttt{gvysl15}, \texttt{gvysl20}) has room of 0.3--1\% of a linear ceiling of 0.7--1.4 and was stopped; six perfectly observed groups are priced linearly.

\noindent\textbf{The U in size.} At 0.28 the U is below the bar where the ladder works: \texttt{bvyu4} room 18--121\%, $+0.014$ ($t$ 1.3, seven of ten); \texttt{vyu4} room 15--55\%, $+0.010$ ($t$ 1.5, seven of ten); \texttt{gvyu4} room 1--5\%, stopped. Two things hold it down. The compensation is referenced to the first type, here the small exposed one, whose positive exposure is discounted under $Q$, so every type was scaled to that low value; at 0.35 BGN's small type had no positive-NPV project and the gate rejected it (\texttt{bvyu5}); the reordered versions (\texttt{bvyu5r}, \texttt{bvyu6r}, \texttt{vyu4r}, \texttt{vyu5r}) put the unexposed level-1.0 type first. And, structurally, a one-signed exposure in a type ladder is revealed by the compensation itself: the compensated cash-flow level, hence ROE, ranks the types by $|b|$, and without compensation book-to-market does; the sign-flip hump escapes because its premium is non-monotone in the exposure. The reordered ladder shows it: in BGN the U reaches the bar at 0.35 (\texttt{bvyu5r}: room 8--27\% of a linear ceiling that rose from 0.25 to 0.64, $+0.0295$, $t$ 3.1, nine of ten) and falls back at 0.42 (\texttt{bvyu6r}: $+0.025$, $t$ 2.6, eight of ten); in KP14 it has no gap at any loading (\texttt{vyu4r} $+0.003$, \texttt{vyu5r} $+0.000$), the compensation revealing the one-signed exposure completely.

\noindent\textbf{The U in leverage.} The most natural non-monotone story: highly levered firms in distress and lowly levered firms credit-rationed, both more exposed than the middle. On GS21's fixed-coupon chassis (\texttt{gvyzlf}: coupons 0.15 / 0.40 / 0.70 for life, D/E about 0.25 / 1.0 / 3.25, where the endogenous capital structure spans 0.002--0.006 across quintiles) a production-level exposure of 0.3 on the two outer types (\texttt{gvylu30}; \texttt{gvylu30c} value-compensated) has no room: 1--3\% and 0.3--0.5\% of linear ceilings of 0.5--1.4. With leverage fixed by type the exposure is a function of three perfectly observed groups, and a linear model in six characteristics fits three group means through leverage and book-to-market; room needs a second factor varying continuously within the type.

\noindent\textbf{Earlier attempts.} Before the ladder, putting the non-monotonicity into the exposure itself failed in both models. \texttt{bvyvee} gives BGN five types on a visible level ladder ($\theta_f$ = 0.6 to 1.4 multiplying the cash-flow level) with the exposure V-shaped across it ($b_f$ = $+0.07$, 0, $-0.07$, 0, $+0.07$): room 23\%, gap $+0.007$ ($t$ 1.3), because the level itself moves returns monotonically ($-0.15$ to $+1.0$\% a month along the ladder) and swamps the V. In GS21 a $y$ exposure quadratic in the firm's standardized coupon, $c(\tilde b^2-1)$ with $c$ = $+0.1$, $+0.2$, $-0.1$ (\texttt{gvyql10}, \texttt{gvyql20}, \texttt{gvyqh10}), gives room of 1 to 11\% and gaps of $-0.002$, $-0.002$, $-0.026$: the leverage characteristic spans only 0.002 to 0.006 across quintiles, so the term has almost nothing to load on, and the one-type wiring hands the market a negative premium.
