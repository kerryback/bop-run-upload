# The OU state `y` in KP14: what it changes in the pricing, and what the code does about it

**Who this is for.** Alexander, coming to this cold. It is self-contained on the theory and points at
the exact lines to open. It deliberately does not assume familiarity with the `variants/` pipeline.

**The one-sentence version.** Adding a priced *mean-reverting* state to KP14 is legitimate, but the
risk adjustment cannot be carried the way KP14 carries its own — as a constant addition to the
discount rate — because for a mean-reverting state the Girsanov adjustment **saturates** rather than
accumulating linearly in the horizon; our code carries it the KP14 way, and the resulting prices are
not generated by any constant price of `y`-risk.

**Status.** The defect is established analytically, confirmed by Monte Carlo, and now measured on the
protocol panel. Nothing has been re-solved and no number in [docs/RESULTS.md](docs/RESULTS.md) has
been changed. The decision about what to do is what we need to talk about; §8 lists the questions.

---

## 1. The model as KP14 wrote it, and why its shape is exact

Kogan–Papanikolaou (2014, *JF* 69(2)) has two priced aggregate shocks, both geometric Brownian
motions:

- `x`, disembodied productivity, priced at `gamma_x`;
- `z`, investment-specific technology, priced at `gamma_z`.

The SDF is `dM/M = -r dt - gamma_x dB_x - gamma_z dB_z`, with constant prices of risk.

A project's assets-in-place value is a claim to a flow proportional to `x_t`, and the paper's
equation (11) prices it with a **constant**:

```
A = 1 / (r + gamma_x*sigma_x + delta - mu_x + theta_c)
```

The risk premium appears as `gamma_x*sigma_x` **added to the discount rate**. This is not an
approximation. For a GBM, the change of measure is a constant shift in the drift — under Q,
`mu_x -> mu_x - gamma_x*sigma_x`, at every horizon — so the cumulative risk adjustment to
`log x` over a horizon `t` is exactly `-gamma_x*sigma_x*t`, **linear in `t`**. A constant addition to
a discount rate is precisely a linear-in-`t` adjustment to the cumulant. The two are the same object.

**Keep that sentence in mind.** Everything below is a consequence of it being false for an OU
process.

## 2. What the `vy` route adds, and why

We added a third priced aggregate state `y`, an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
([parameters_kp14.py:24](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L24),
[:94](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L94)):

```
dy = -kappa_y * y dt + sigma_y * dB_v        kappa_y = 0.35,  sigma_y = sqrt(2*kappa_y) = 0.83666
```

`sigma_y` is set so the stationary standard deviation of `y` is exactly 1, which makes `y` readable in
units of its own unconditional sd. The SDF gains a third term, `- gamma_v dB_v`, with `gamma_v` a
constant price of `y`-risk ([:28](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L28)).

Firms differ in how much they load on it. Firm `n` is assigned a type `f` with exposure `beta_f`, and
its cash flows are scaled by `exp(beta_f * y_t)`
([panel_functions_kp14.py:121-124](variants/kp_vy/panel_functions_kp14.py#L121-L124)). In the two
economies at issue the ladder is `beta_f in {0.02, 0.07, 0.14}`.

**Why we wanted this.** The rest of the project's economies vary a *price of risk* or an *exposure
ladder* — both of which shift the cross-sectional map from characteristics to expected returns
without changing its shape. An exponential loading on a persistent state does something different: it
makes each firm's exposure *depend on the state*, so the map **bends** as `y` moves rather than
sliding. That is the property we were after, and `vyx` / `vyg25` are the only two economies in the
project that produce a large complexity gap (fair gap `+0.1251` and `+0.1636`, against `-0.008` to
`+0.0034` everywhere else).

So the OU state is doing exactly the economic work it was introduced to do. The question is whether
it is being **priced** correctly.

## 3. The theory: what mean reversion changes

Consider the object the assets-in-place coefficient has to be. Fix a type with loading `b = beta_f`,
and write `rho` for the effective discount rate `r + gamma_x*sigma_x + delta - mu_x` (`= 0.2297` at
our parameters). The value of a claim to the flow `e^{b y_s}` is

```
V(y)  =  E^Q_y [ integral_0^inf  e^{-rho t}  e^{b y_t}  dt ]
```

where the expectation is under the **risk-neutral** measure. Girsanov for `dB_v^Q = dB_v + gamma_v dt`
gives the Q-dynamics of `y`:

```
dy = ( -kappa_y*y  -  gamma_v*sigma_y ) dt  +  sigma_y dB_v^Q
```

Mean reversion is unchanged; the **long-run mean shifts** from 0 to `-gamma_v*sigma_y/kappa_y`.

### 3.1 The linear-versus-saturating point

Under P, `E[y_t | y_0] = y_0 e^{-kappa t}`. Under Q it is
`y_0 e^{-kappa t} - (gamma_v*sigma_y/kappa)(1 - e^{-kappa t})`. So the risk adjustment to the
conditional mean of `b*y_t` is

```
-  b * gamma_v * sigma_y * (1 - e^{-kappa_y t}) / kappa_y          <-- SATURATES at b*gamma_v*sigma_y/kappa_y
```

whereas the KP14 shape — a constant `b*gamma_v*sigma_y` added to the discount rate — is equivalent to

```
-  b * gamma_v * sigma_y * t                                        <-- GROWS WITHOUT BOUND
```

Since `(1 - e^{-kappa t})/kappa < t` for every `t > 0`, with equality only at `t = 0`, the
constant-rate version **always over-discounts**, and the gap widens with horizon. It saturates on a
timescale `1/kappa_y = 2.86` years, while the claim's effective duration is roughly
`1/rho = 4.35` years — so a large share of the claim's value sits at horizons where the two have
already parted company.

A back-of-envelope for the size: weighting the two adjustments by `rho*e^{-rho t}` and taking the
ratio gives `rho/(rho + kappa_y) = 0.396`. The correct cumulative risk adjustment is about **40%** of
what the constant-rate shape charges — the code over-charges the `y` premium by a factor of roughly
**2.5**. (This is intuition, not the measurement; §7 has the measurements.)

### 3.2 The equation the coefficient must satisfy

Writing `V = e^{b y} A(y)` and substituting into the Feynman–Kac equation `rho V = e^{b y} + L^Q V`
with `L^Q f = (-kappa*y - gamma_v*sigma_y) f' + 0.5*sigma_y^2 f''` gives

```
[ rho + kappa_y*y*b + gamma_v*sigma_y*b - 0.5*sigma_y^2*b^2 ] A   -   L^Q A   -   sigma_y^2*b*A'   =   1
```

Three pieces, and it matters which is which:

1. the **discount bracket**, which contains the Ito term `kappa_y*y*b`, the convexity term
   `-0.5*sigma_y^2*b^2`, and the premium `gamma_v*sigma_y*b`;
2. the **operator** `L^Q`, which carries the Girsanov drift `-gamma_v*sigma_y`;
3. the **Ito cross term** `sigma_y^2*b*A'`, from the covariance between `e^{b y}` and `A(y)`.

Note the premium appears in *two* places: once in the bracket and once inside `L^Q`. That is the
whole story. In the GBM case there is no `A'` — `A` is a constant — so the operator and cross terms
vanish and only the bracket survives, which is why (11) is exact there.

## 4. What the code actually solves

### 4.1 Assets in place

The generator is built with the **physical** drift
([parameters_kp14.py:101-117](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L101-L117)):

```python
def _build_Qy():
    drift = -kappa_y * y_grid            # <-- line 103: physical. No -gamma_v*sigma_y.
    ...                                  # upwind differencing, REFLECTING boundaries
Qy = _build_Qy()
```

The discount vector is built at
[parameters_kp14.py:122-126](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L122-L126):

```python
def const_ty(f):
    b = type_bv[f]
    return const_base_y + b*gamma_v*sigma_y + b*kappa_y*y_grid - 0.5*b**2*sigma_y**2
```

and the coefficient is the resolvent
([parameters_kp14.py:128-133](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L128-L133)):

```python
def _solve_coeff_ty(f, theta_c):
    return np.linalg.solve(np.diag(const_ty(f) + theta_c) - Qy, np.ones(NY))
```

So the code solves

```
[ rho + kappa_y*y*b + gamma_v*sigma_y*b - 0.5*sigma_y^2*b^2 ] A   -   L^P A   =   1
```

**Line up the two equations.** The discount bracket is *exactly right*, term for term. What differs:

| | correct | code |
|---|---|---|
| discount bracket | `rho + kappa*y*b + gamma_v*sigma_y*b - 0.5*sigma_y^2*b^2` | identical |
| operator | `L^Q` (drift `-kappa*y - gamma_v*sigma_y`) | `L^P` (drift `-kappa*y`) |
| Ito cross term | `- sigma_y^2*b*A'` | absent |

The omitted term is therefore

```
( gamma_v*sigma_y  -  sigma_y^2*b ) * A'(y)
```

and the two pieces are not comparable in size: at `gamma_v = 1.8`, `gamma_v*sigma_y = 1.506`, while
`sigma_y^2*b = 0.098` at the largest exposure. **The Girsanov drift is the dominant omission, about
15x the Ito cross term.** (An earlier draft of the internal note had this backwards and named only
the small term.)

### 4.2 The growth option has the same defect

This is easy to miss. The growth-option value `G` is solved by a separate finite-difference routine,
and it uses the **same physical generator**
([kp14_fd_vy.py:29-32](variants/kp_vy/kp14_fd_vy.py#L29-L32)):

```python
for jy in range(NY):
    if jy != iy:
        Qs[2*iy,     2*jy    ] += Qy[iy, jy]      # <-- Qy again: physical
        Qs[2*iy + 1, 2*jy + 1] += Qy[iy, jy]
```

with its discount vector `rho_ty`
([parameters_kp14.py:151-156](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L151-L156)) carrying the premium the
same constant-rate way:

```python
rho_ty = np.stack([( r + gamma_x*gm_grid*sigma_x - mu_x
                   + type_bv[f]*gamma_v*sigma_y            # <-- line 152, constant-rate again
                   + type_bv[f]*kappa_y*y_grid
                   - 0.5*type_bv[f]**2*sigma_y**2
                   - alpha/(1-alpha)*(mu_z - gamma_z*sigma_z - 0.5*sigma_z**2)
                   - 0.5*(alpha/(1-alpha))**2*sigma_z**2 ) for f in range(ntypes)])
```
The `z` block on the last two lines is a GBM term and is exact. The `y` block on line 152 is not.

So **both** halves of firm value — assets in place via `A`, and the growth option via `G` — inherit
the same specification error. The integral tables in
[integ_kp14.py:12-13](variants/kp_vy/integ_kp14.py#L12-L13) and
[:29-30](variants/kp_vy/integ_kp14.py#L29-L30) are built on top of `A` and `G` and add nothing new;
they inherit whatever is wrong upstream.

### 4.3 What is *correctly* physical, and should stay that way

A natural first reaction is "so make everything risk-neutral". That would be wrong. These are
simulation objects and are correctly under P:

- the simulated path of `y`
  ([panel_functions_kp14.py:37-43](variants/kp_vy/panel_functions_kp14.py#L37-L43)) — the economy is
  realised under P;
- the Gauss–Hermite nodes used for the conditional expectation `E_t[R_{t+1}]`
  ([panel_functions_kp14.py:48](variants/kp_vy/panel_functions_kp14.py#L48),
  [sdf_compute_kp14.py:105-107](variants/kp_vy/sdf_compute_kp14.py#L105-L107)) — `erets` is a
  *physical* expected return and must be;
- the one-period helper `Ey_exp`
  ([parameters_kp14.py:159-163](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L159-L163)), explicitly labelled
  physical.

The measure only needs to be Q inside the **pricing functions** `A` and `G`. That is the distinction
the current code loses.

## 5. What is right without the OU, and why that matters

Set `beta_f = 0` (this is the `kpbase` economy, `type_bv = [0.0]`, `gamma_v = 0`). Then `const_ty`
collapses to `const_base_y`, a constant across `y`-nodes, and since `Qy` is a generator its rows sum
to zero, so `Qy @ 1 = 0` (verified: max abs `8.9e-16`). The resolvent therefore returns

```
A = [diag(c) - Qy]^{-1} 1 = (1/c) * 1
```

i.e. **exactly** KP14's equation (11). With `beta_f = 0` nothing in the model depends on `y` at all,
the omitted term `(gamma_v*sigma_y - sigma_y^2*b)*A'` is identically zero because `A' = 0` *and*
`b = 0`, and the code is the published model.

This is not just a reassuring remark — it is the experimental control. It means any pricing error we
measure at `beta_f > 0` is attributable to the `y` channel and not to the grid, the CIR
discretisation, the `G` solve, or anything else shared with the baseline. §7 uses it exactly that way.

## 6. A device that does *not* rescue this

`bv_comp` / `type_theta`
([parameters_kp14.py:136-140](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L136-L140)) rescales each type's
coefficients so that all types have the same value at `y = 0`:

```python
type_theta = (A0_ty[0, _i0] / A0_ty[:, _i0]) ** bv_comp
A0_ty = A0_ty * type_theta[:, None]; ...
```

It was introduced so that types differ in *exposure* rather than in *size*, and it does absorb much
of the level distortion — at `bv_comp = 1.2` the post-compensation level errors are about
0.93 / 0.96 / 0.97 rather than 0.93 / 0.82 / 0.75.

**It cannot fix a return-level error, and the reason is structural.** `type_theta` multiplies `A`,
which feeds optimal project capital `K_j = (alpha*z*A)^{1/(1-alpha)}`
([panel_functions_kp14.py:156](variants/kp_vy/panel_functions_kp14.py#L156)); price, book and cash
flow then all end up proportional to `type_theta^{1/(1-alpha)}`. It is a change of units per type.
Returns are ratios, so it passes straight through them. What survives compensation — and what the
measurements pick up — is the `y`-*shape* of `A`, not its level.

(Loose end while we are here: `pm_tau` at
[parameters_kp14.py:138](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L138) is computed and never used
anywhere. Harmless, but it should either be used or deleted.)

## 7. How wrong: three independent measurements

### 7.1 The coefficient itself

Solving the correct Feynman–Kac equation in closed form and comparing to what the module computes
(`vyx` parameters, `gamma_v = 1.8`):

| `b` | `A_code / A_exact` | `A_code /` constant-rate closed form |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | **1.000** | — |
| 0.02 | 0.931 | 1.0006 |
| 0.07 | 0.823 | 1.005 |
| 0.14 | **0.748** | 1.015 |

The left column is the error: claim values understated by 6.9%, 17.7% and 25.2%, and by **exactly
zero** at `b = 0`. The right column is the diagnosis: the code reproduces the *constant-rate* formula
to within 0.06–1.5%, the residual being the small Ito term. Independently confirmed by Monte Carlo
under Q: **3.1028** (se 0.0005) against a closed form of **3.0982** at `b = 0.14`, `y = 0`. The
21-node grid is not the issue — 21 nodes and 2001 nodes agree to four significant figures.

Reproduce: `python variants/kp_vy/check_y_risk_adjustment.py`.

### 7.2 Whether any single price of `y`-risk prices the model

The model states three constant prices of risk, so its own prices must satisfy, firm by firm and
month by month,

```
E^Q_t[ P_{i,t+1} + CF_{i,t+1} ] / P_{i,t}  =  exp(r*dt)
```

This needs no elasticity approximation — it re-takes the module's own expected-payoff terms under Q
(`mu_x -> mu_x - gamma_x*sigma_x`, `mu_z -> mu_z - gamma_z*sigma_z`, and the `y` nodes displaced by
the exact one-month OU Girsanov shift `-gamma_v*sigma_y*(1-e^{-kappa_y*dt})/kappa_y`). Run on the
protocol panel, 485 months, 219,295 firm-months. Percentages a year are 12x the monthly figure.

| | `kpbase` | `vyx` (`gamma_v` 1.8) | `vyg25` (`gamma_v` 2.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `beta_f` = 0 | **+0.016 %/yr** | — | — |
| `beta_f` = 0.02 | — | +2.59 %/yr | +3.53 %/yr |
| `beta_f` = 0.07 | — | +7.85 %/yr | +10.34 %/yr |
| `beta_f` = 0.14 | — | **+13.33 %/yr** | **+16.97 %/yr** |

`kpbase` at +0.016 %/yr is the control from §5: that is the entire discretisation error of this
machinery with the `y` channel switched off. Everything else is the `y` channel.

Solving for the `gamma_v` that would zero each type's mispricing gives **13.55 / 7.93 / 5.52** in
`vyx` (declared 1.8) and **16.67 / 9.22 / 6.39** in `vyg25` (declared 2.5), monotone in `beta_f`, and
the spread does not close out to `gamma_v = 20`. The low-exposure type cannot be priced by *any*
`gamma_v`, because it has too little `y`-exposure for the price to have leverage on — the signature of
a **shape** error rather than a level error.

About half of each exposed type's expected excess return, and **52–58% of the cross-type premium
spread**, is compensation for no priced risk at all.

### 7.3 What it does to the attainable Sharpe ratio

Using `vyg25`'s saved conditional moments (same solve ids the results table reports) and stripping
expected returns of the part no priced factor explains, on the 125-month evaluation window:

| | as reported | stripped |
|---|---|---|
| `SR_max` | 1.4697 | 0.5996 |
| `SR_orth` (what the equal-weighted market does *not* span) | 1.4026 | 0.5527 |

Roughly **three fifths** of the ceiling, and of the part the market does not span, is expected return
the model's own SDF does not price.

### 7.4 A caution on reading the two "implied price" numbers together

§7.1's internal note quotes an implied price of `y`-risk of 4.18 / 3.68 / 3.26; §7.2 quotes
13.55 / 7.93 / 5.52. These are **different diagnostics and need not agree**. The first asks what
constant `lambda` makes the correct Feynman–Kac equation hold pointwise given the code's `A` — a local
ODE residual on one claim. The second asks what `gamma_v` would reconcile realised expected *returns*
with the SDF, forcing the whole error through a single channel with limited leverage. Both reject a
common price across types; neither is "the" implied price, because the model's prices are simply not
generated by any constant-`gamma_v` SDF.

## 8. The fix, and the constraint that makes it expensive

The fix is one line of specification: build the generator with the Q-drift, per type, keeping the
discount vector as it is —

```
drift  =  -kappa_y*y  -  gamma_v*sigma_y  +  sigma_y^2*b
```

`Qy` becomes one generator per type, which costs nothing computationally (`kp14_fd_vy.py` already
solves per type).

**It runs straight into a grid problem, and this is the part worth your judgement.** Under Q the
long-run mean of `y` is `-gamma_v*sigma_y/kappa_y`:

| | Q long-run mean of `y` | grid | `const_ty` hits zero at |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vyx` (`gamma_v` 1.8) | **-4.30** | `[-3.5, +3.5]` | `y = -8.85` (at `b = 0.14`) |
| `vyg25` (`gamma_v` 2.5) | **-5.98** | `[-3.5, +3.5]` | `y = -10.52` |

The risk-neutral mean of `y` **lies outside the current grid entirely**, and the boundaries are
*reflecting* ([parameters_kp14.py:101-117](variants/kp_vy/parameters_kp14.py#L101-L117)). Under P,
`+/-3.5` is 3.5 stationary sd and reflection is harmless. Under Q the process would sit on the lower
boundary. A correct solve needs `y_max` around 7 (convergence to the closed form is reached there to
four figures), which takes `NY` from 21 to about 41 at the current spacing.

And it cannot simply be widened without limit: `const_ty` goes negative below `-8.85` (`vyx`) and
`-10.52` (`vyg25`), and a negative discount removes the operator's dissipation and makes the
resolvent ill-posed. So the window between "wide enough to contain the Q-measure" and "ill-posed" is
narrow, and it is **narrower at higher `gamma_v`** — i.e. narrower in exactly the economy with the
larger reported gap. Avoiding ever having to solve under Q is very likely why the constant-discount
route was taken in the first place.

Changing `NY` is a change in solve precision, which the project's protocol holds uniform within a
model, so **all three KP14 economies re-solve together**: three `G` solves, `ntypes * NY` integral
tables (63 files becomes about 123), then 30 seed jobs re-run. Six committed solve ids move.

## 9. Questions for us

1. **Do we fix it, or do we retire the `vy` route?** The fix is cheap to write and expensive to
   adopt, and we do not know what the corrected economies report. Everything measured so far says how
   much of the *current* ceiling is not a risk premium; it does not say what the *corrected* model's
   ceiling is. The corrected model has a different generator, a wider grid, different exposures and a
   different covariance matrix.
2. **Is the OU state viable at these prices of risk at all?** The Q-mean sitting 4.3 to 6.0
   stationary sd below the P-mean, against an ill-posedness wall at 8.9 to 10.5, is tight. If we want
   a large `gamma_v` *and* a large `beta_f` ladder, we may be asking for an economy whose risk-neutral
   measure the numerical method cannot represent. Worth deciding before paying for the re-solve.
3. **Is the exponential loading the right functional form?** `exp(beta_f*y)` is what makes the
   exposure map bend, which is the economics we wanted. But it is also what makes the claim's value a
   nontrivial function of `y` and hence what breaks the constant-discount shape. A loading that kept
   `A` constant would price exactly and would also deliver none of the mechanism. This is the real
   trade-off and it is a modelling choice, not a bug.
4. **What do we say in the paper about KP14's own construction?** Our (11)-shaped discount is
   inherited from the paper and is exact there. The failure is entirely in the extension. Worth being
   explicit so a referee does not read it as a criticism of KP14.
5. **Scope.** Should the correctness check in §7.2 become a standing test for every economy we add,
   not just the KP14 ones? It is cheap and it caught this.
